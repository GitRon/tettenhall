{% comment %} The men the player decides about, one line per man, in columns he can compare down: his own war band and his own cells. The rows are the slot, each a "". A captive is a man he may be about to take into the roster, so he compares him by the same figures - which is why the two share one table: a column added for one is a column the other gets. Somebody else's men keep "": the player knows too little about them to fill most of these columns. On a phone each man is a record - see docs/patterns/responsive-layout.md: the name across the top with the row menu beside it, the short figures two to a line, the gauges and the gear one to a line. On a desk narrower than the table it scrolls inside its own box rather than dragging the page sideways. The box is positioned so that it contains the visually hidden column heads and the row menu: an absolutely positioned element escapes an overflow box that is not its containing block, and that one would widen the page. {% endcomment %}
