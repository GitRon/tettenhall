{% comment %} One man, as a row: the lists that show somebody else's warriors side by side - a rival's roster, the captives a rival holds, the mercenaries in a pub. The player's own war band and his own cells are a table instead, "", because those are the men he decides about and compares down a column. What the row carries is what decides a month: level, renown, health and morale, wage, the gear he stands in, and whether he can fight. Everything else - culture, strength and dexterity, injuries, what a blade rolls - is one click away on his own page, which the name leads to and which answers every relation this row is rendered for. Renown is here because it decides who takes a leader's seat: on a rival's roster, who leads them once their leader falls, and in a pub, where a man hired joins the player's own line of succession at the place his renown gives him. What may be known is not this template's to decide: "knowledge" arrives from the list, and the gauge and the gear follow it - exact figures for his own men, a bucket for everybody else, and no gear at all for a rival's. See docs/patterns/warrior-knowledge.md. "is_pub" says the list is a pub, where "is_player_faction" decides whether there is anything to press. Nothing else drawn as a row has a control. A phone gets the portrait and the name on one line, the tags across the whole row below, and the controls under them - squeezed beside a control, the tags stack one to a line. A desk puts the controls on the name's line. Set left explicitly: a rival's page centres the column it sits in. {% endcomment %}
